# How to get a look without naming an artist

If you can only name the effect by naming a person, you have not finished describing it yet. That
sentence is the whole lesson; everything else is why, and how.

## Why the name fails you

Naming a living artist gives you exactly one dial, and you cannot turn it. You cannot warm the
palette without warming everything, change the crop without changing the medium, or carry the look
to a different model next year. And the name is the one prompt habit that adds legal exposure
without adding craft: it is refused outright on some models, silently deleted on others, and it
converts a copyright question, where style is generally free, into a trademark and endorsement
question, where it is not.

## The decomposition

Take the reference. Ask what is actually producing the feeling you want. Write those things down.
Recombine them with your own subject and staging.

**Before:** `in the style of [living illustrator], a fox in a forest`

**After:**

> Gouache and coloured pencil on cold-press paper, visible tooth in the flat areas. Limited
> palette of burnt sienna, sage and bone, with one saturated vermilion accent. Flat compressed
> space, no cast shadows, forms separated by outline weight rather than by depth. Confident varied
> line, thick on the shadow side. A fox mid-stride between birch trunks, cropped tight, generous
> negative space at the top of the frame.

That is nine independent controls where the name was one. More controllable, portable between
models, stable across versions, and outside both the legal theories that names create.

## The axes to look along

For images: medium and substrate, mark-making, palette, lighting, lens, composition, era, mood.
For sound: instrumentation, arrangement, tempo and feel, harmony, timbre, mix, vocal delivery,
production era, genre, and genres are genres, not people.

When a name slips into a brief anyway, the Compliance Pass offers this same scaffold in place:
fill the axes you can see, and the prompt improves as the finding clears. If you want a consistent
look across a set, the sanctioned mechanisms, your own reference images, a random style code ,
do it with no name at all.

Try it: the brief below is the fox, fully decomposed. Strike it, then change one axis at a time ,
the palette, the crop, the line, and watch each dial move independently. That independence is the
thing the name could never give you.
